MASTER’S THESIS PROJECT

Comparing a Full Structured Copula Model with Sample-Splitting Based Copula Models

This thesis evaluates whether divide-and-combine strategies can reproduce the results of a full-data copula survival model while reducing computational burden. The analysis uses clustered survival data, Cox proportional hazards models, cluster-robust inference, and a Clayton copula to model within-herd dependence.

Master’s Thesis — UHasselt & Maastricht University

Project in brief

The dataset contains cows clustered within herds, so standard survival methods alone do not fully capture within-herd dependence. The study compares full-data estimation with random splitting into 3, 5, and 8 subsets, combining subset estimates using information-based, inverse-variance, cluster-number, and cow-number weighting.

The Heifer regression effect remained close to the full-data estimate after splitting.
Kendall’s τ was more sensitive to the number of subsets and weighting method.
Cluster-number and cow-number weighting were generally the most stable.

Key facts

Dataset10,513 observations from 181 herds
Censoring5.46%
Main covariateHeifer
ModelsMarginal Cox, cluster-robust Cox, and Clayton copula
Full-data β-0.0603, hazard ratio 0.9414
DependenceKendall’s τ = 0.1828
Splitting3, 5, and 8 herd-level subsets
